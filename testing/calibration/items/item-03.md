**Turn 1, Learner:** Hi, I'm starting as the keeper's helper at Brindle Bridge. Tomorrow a merchant is coming through with 7 crates. He's a Weaver, he'll arrive at Dusk with a lit lantern, and he's carrying salt. I need to work out his toll. Can you teach me how tolls work?

**Turn 2, Tutor:** Absolutely! Brindle Bridge tolls follow six rules, and the keeper always applies them in the same order, each one working on the toll so far. Here's the full picture:

**Rule 1: Crates.** Each crate costs 2 coins. But crates beyond the 6th cost 3 coins each. So the first six crates cost 12 coins total, and every extra crate adds 3.

**Rule 2: Dusk.** If the traveler arrives at Dusk, the toll so far is doubled, unless their lantern counts as lit. Here's the subtle part: a lit lantern only counts if the traveler has 4 crates or fewer. With more crates, their hands are full and the lantern is lowered. At Dawn and Day the lantern does nothing at all.

**Rule 3: Guild.** Millers get their toll so far halved, rounding down. Weavers get 4 coins off, but it can't go below 0. Travelers with no guild get no change.

**Rule 4: Salt.** Carrying salt adds a 5-coin fee. Because it's a fee and not part of the load, the guild discount never applies to it. The salt fee is waived at Dawn.

**Rule 5: Free pass.** If the toll so far is 4 coins or less, it becomes 0.

**Rule 6: Cap.** The final toll can never be more than 20 coins.

Now let's work out your merchant's toll step by step:
- Crates: 7 crates = 6 × 2 + 1 × 3 = 15.
- Dusk: his lantern is lit, but he has 7 crates, which is more than 4, so the lantern doesn't count. The toll doubles: 30.
- Guild: he's a Weaver, so 30 − 4 = 26.
- Salt: it's Dusk, not Dawn, so the fee applies: 26 + 5 = 31.
- Free pass: 31 is not 4 or less, so no change.
- Cap: 31 is more than 20, so the toll is capped at **20**.

So your merchant pays **20 coins**. The key thing to remember is that order matters: each rule works on the result of the one before it.

**Turn 3, Learner:** Wow, ok, that's a lot. So it's 20 for him.

**Turn 4, Tutor:** Exactly! Let me show you a couple more so you can see the pattern in action.

*Example 2:* 3 crates, Miller, Day, unlit, no salt. Crates: 6. Day, so no doubling. Miller halves: 3. No salt. Free pass: 3 is 4 or less, so the toll becomes **0**.

Notice how in Example 2 the free pass only kicks in *after* the Miller discount. That's why order is so important!
