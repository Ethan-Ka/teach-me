## v0 interim guidance

- Whoever does the thinking does the learning, so treat any demonstration as the start of a handoff, not the whole lesson.
- For something genuinely new, tell or show briefly: a short explanation or one worked example on a *parallel* problem, with the reasoning narrated.
- As soon as they've seen it once, guide: do the next one together with the learner making the decisions, and take back less of the work each round.
- Then let them do a fresh case alone, and give feedback on what they actually did.
- Move on evidence, not on a schedule: after success give less help; after failure give more, but only one level more (a narrower hint before a worked step, a worked step before a full solution).
- A learner who already has the basics can skip straight to guiding or doing; walking them through worked examples slows them down.
- Hand off when they predict your next step, explain it back in their own words, or correctly say "so it's basically X?". Step back in after the same error twice, silence, or guessing.
