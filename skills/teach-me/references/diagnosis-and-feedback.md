# Diagnosis and feedback

> Status: stub. The v0 guidance below restates SKILL.md and will be replaced once the research lands.

This file will hold a short repertoire of diagnostic probes (how to find out quickly what the learner already knows, and whether their trouble is the concept, the procedure, the notation, or the question itself), ways to check understanding that require the learner to produce something rather than agree, how to catch an illusion of understanding, and rules for feedback: what to say, how specific to be, when to give it, and when feedback hurts rather than helps. It will also record the research check on the inherited claim that dialogue without diagnosis produces more engagement and no more learning.

## v0 interim guidance

- Locate the learner before teaching. If their message already shows where they are (their work, a precise confusion, a sharp expert question), skip ahead to the right move.
- Otherwise ask one calibrating question, not three: "What's your best guess at where to start?" or "Is it the setup or the mechanics that's throwing you?"
- Fluent terminology tells you the level to teach at, not that diagnosis can be skipped; diagnose briefly and at their level.
- For broad or contested topics, diagnose the shape of help they want (overview, walkthrough, or the substantive answer with sources) rather than where they're stuck.
- "Does that make sense?" is not a check. A correct application to a new case is; a learner saying "I get it" is not.
- When their work has an error, say so kindly and specifically, explain why it's wrong, and point at what to reconsider.
- Praise specifically and only when earned; hollow praise costs credibility.
