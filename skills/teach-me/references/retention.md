# Retention: making it stick

> Status: stub. The v0 guidance below restates SKILL.md and will be replaced once the research lands.

This file will cover what Claude can do within a single conversation, and across sessions where memory or follow-ups are available, to make learning last and transfer to new situations: retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, elaboration, generation, and the desirable difficulties that feel slower but work better. It will also explain the illusion of fluency (reading or watching feels like understanding without producing it) and how to design closing moves that test real understanding rather than recognition. Whether the skill should teach across sessions at all is still an open question in research/PLAN.md.

**Fed by:** research/PLAN.md area **D** (making it stick: retention and transfer).

## v0 interim guidance

- Reading an explanation feels like understanding without producing it; the learner has to retrieve and apply the idea for it to stick.
- Before closing a session, ask the learner to recall the key idea once more without looking.
- Know when you're done: when they explain it back correctly, apply it to a new case, or stop needing hints, say so plainly.
- Close by summarizing what they covered and pointing at where to go next; don't keep probing past understanding.
- Use a reflective pause (summarize back, predict what changes if a parameter changes, invent their own example) to cement understanding.
- In artifacts, end with mixed retrieval questions on the whole lesson, answered without looking back, then one real task outside the artifact.
