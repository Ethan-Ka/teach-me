Any note on the two bass strings

For you if you strum a few open chords and still count up from the headstock to find a note. One objective, about a week of 15-minute sessions.

Objective. When the page calls a note (natural, sharp or flat), you play it on the low E or A string, frets 0 to 12, on the next downbeat at 60 bpm: eight calls in a row.

    Placement

    Four checks. The second one you answer on the guitar, so have it in your lap.

    1. Guess first

    The note names run A B C D E F G, then start again at A. One fret raises a note by a half step.

    Listen to the difference. E to F is one fret; F to G is two.

    Only two pairs of neighbours are one fret apart: E to F and B to C. Every other pair is two frets apart. That one rule, plus the open string's name, gives you every note on the string.

    2. Walk the low E string

    Start at the open string and apply the rule letter by letter. The diagram is drawn like tab: the higher string (A) on top, the nut on the left.

    Low E, worked out

    The dots on your neck at frets 3, 5, 7 and 12 land on here. Those four are worth memorising first; every other natural is one step from one of them.

    3. The A string, with less help

    Same rule, new starting letter. First fill one gap, then find every copy of a note on both strings.

    Find them on the board

    Click or tap a spot; with a keyboard, Tab onto the board, move with the arrow keys and press Enter.

    4. Sharps and flats

    A sharp (#) is one fret above its letter; a flat (b) is one fret below. F# on the low E is F plus one: fret . Bb on the A string is B minus one: fret .

    One catch: a flat of the open note. Ab on the A string would be fret minus one, so use the A at fret 12 and come back one: fret .

    5. On the guitar, against the click

    This is the objective, and the page can't hear you, so the check is built from things you can observe. Each bar, the page shows a note. Find it and play it on beat 1 of the next bar. On that same beat the page plays the right note, so you hear at once whether yours matched. For the first few runs, keep a tuner app open as a second opinion.

    A clean call means the right note, on the downbeat, without stopping. Pass a rung with eight clean calls in a row. Record one run on your phone and listen back: what you hear while playing hides late notes.
