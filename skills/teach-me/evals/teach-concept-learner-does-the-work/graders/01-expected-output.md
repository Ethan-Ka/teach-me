The result matches this description: The response teaches rather than demonstrates. Before explaining, it briefly diagnoses where the learner is (for example, asking what they picture rebase doing to their commits, or what happens at the moment a conflict appears), with one calibrating question rather than several, or it explicitly builds on what the learner already said (comfortable with merge and branches, confused by conflicts and --continue/--skip, lost commits once). It does not open with a wall-of-text tutorial or a full command cheat sheet. Any example is a small, parallel case (such as a short commit graph) rather than a complete walkthrough of the learner's own rebase done for them. The turn ends by asking the learner to do something substantive, such as predict what the commit graph looks like after a rebase or what --skip will do, so the learner, not the tutor, does the next piece of thinking. The tone is direct and warm without hollow praise.
