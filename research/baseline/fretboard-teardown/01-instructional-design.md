# Fretboard Lesson Book: instructional-design teardown

Artifact: `examples/Fretboard Lesson Book.html` (282 KB; 11 phases, 96 lessons, 118 tab figures, 3 tools).
Graded against: `testing/rubric.md` (G5, D8), `testing/grader-prompt.md`, and SKILL.md "Building instructional artifacts".

Method: I stripped all text to a file and read all of it. I extracted all 118 `<pre>` tab blocks separately, because naive tag-stripping drops everything after a `<-` arrow. I read both script blocks in full (progress/planner; audio, fretboard, metronome, ear trainer). I checked every fret-to-note claim in the tabs by hand against standard tuning. Screenshots were taken with headless Chrome. At 1280px everything is fine. A true 390px test was **not possible**: headless Chrome enforces a 500px minimum window, and the 390 capture is a crop of a 500px layout. At 500px a DOM probe showed no page-level horizontal scroll. The only overflow is the 653px fretboard SVG, which scrolls inside its own container.

---

## 1. Verdict

This is a 96-lesson practice syllabus with an answer key that is wrong more than a dozen times. It has no in-page way for the learner to show they can do anything. The sound engine is never used to let the learner hear the one thing each lesson is about. Of the 109 buttons, 96 are "Mark done". The only real learner decision in the page is a bolted-on ear trainer with right/wrong feedback. It contradicts its own lesson: it plays a bare triad, where the lesson says a cadence is needed, and it cannot test the degrees or chord types that the pass criteria require. The tabs are the book's "check your work" mechanism ("Here is C major on each set so you can check your work"), and they are wrong in at least 15 figures. So a self-taught learner following the instructions will check correct work against a wrong key and "fix" it into an error. They cannot find out, because nothing in the artifact can hear them play.

## 2. Rubric score

**G5 — PASS (narrowly, on a technicality).** The artifact does ask the learner to produce and decide things. The ear trainer requires a choice ("A chord establishes the key, then one note sounds. Name its function."). Lessons assign derivations: "Find the higher Am and Bdim yourself as a check on the method" (Harmonizing the scale), and "Build ten more chords from symbols alone… Am6. D9. Ebmaj7. G7b9. C#m7b5." (Chord symbols, decoded). But apart from the ear trainer, all of this production happens off the page and is never captured, checked or answered. In several places the answer is printed directly above the prompt to derive it. In Lesson 3, "Derive each one instead of reading it" comes *after* the full inversion tab. In Lesson 5, "Derive, don't memorize" is followed immediately by "Here is C major on each set."

**D8 — 1 / 4.** The anchor for 1 is "Information plus a quiz bolted on at the end." The lesson sections contain no interaction except a self-report "Mark done" button (96 of the 109 buttons). The only element that checks anything is the ear trainer in the "Tools" box at the top. It is not wired into any lesson. Its feedback is right/wrong plus a generic line: "Not that one. Play it again and listen for what makes the right answer different." The anchor for 2 ("Sections with some interaction") is not met, because the sections themselves have none. The anchor for 3 needs objectives → activation → worked example + exercise + feedback that explains why → final retrieval. Only the first exists: the "Pass when" lines work as decent performance objectives. Activation happens once (Lesson 1: "Go to the test at the bottom first"). There is no explanatory feedback anywhere and no cumulative retrieval. Nothing earns a 2 under "start at 2, quote evidence to move up". Being generous, a reader who counts the off-page drills as "interaction" would give a 2. Strict grading says 1.

(Not in scope but worth noting: under **G2** this artifact would FAIL outright. It states many false fret/note facts and never corrects them. See findings F01–F16.)

## 4. Structural failures (patterns, not one-offs)

1. **Unverified notation used as the answer key.** At least 15 tab figures carry wrong notes, wrong labels or contradictions (F01–F16), all in the same form: fret numbers that don't match the note or degree labels next to them. The book's designated self-check is "compare to the tab" (F17), so these errors defeat the learner's only checking mechanism. The errors are spread through every phase, from Lesson 2 to Phase 10, so this is a process failure: nothing checked any figure. The page contains a correct pitch engine (`OPEN=[40,45,50,55,59,64]`, `NOTES[midi%12]`) that could have validated every figure.

2. **The artifact is not where the practice happens.** Every drill and every check is off-page: tuner app, voice memos, YouTube backing tracks, "have someone". Interactive elements are general-purpose tools (metronome, explore-only fretboard, generic ear trainer) placed in a "Tools" box and not tied to any lesson's objective. The learner's work never comes back into the artifact, so the artifact can't give feedback, can't detect illusions of competence, and can't confirm a pass. The 96 "Mark done" buttons show the result: the design treats self-report as assessment.

3. **Silent instruction for an auditory skill.** In every phase the decisive content is a sound: curl, vibrato, shuffle, behind the beat, b5 passing, the 6th's mood, 7 vs b7, 7#9. Each is described in adjectives ("Bright", "Floating", "a HAIR sharp") and never played, though a synth that can play every figure is already loaded. Perceptual training (the Ear phase) also comes after the lessons whose checks depend on perception.

4. **Show-then-tell-them-to-derive.** Lessons repeatedly print the full answer and then tell the learner to "derive, don't memorize". There is no completion problem, no hidden answer, and no visible fading. The handoff from worked example to independent work depends entirely on the learner's discipline.

5. **Accretion without consolidation.** The book was revised by adding lessons ("00" numbering, "Moved up from…", "missing from your plan") and never re-integrated. The results: duplicated lessons with conflicting content (F13, F30), stale counts (F33), contradictory mechanics (F11, F35), a truncated key sentence (F34), and a linear gate that grew to 96 items (F27).

6. **Front-loaded depth, back-loaded lists.** Phases 1–2 get mechanisms, worked examples and checks. Phases 4–8, which carry the skills that are hardest to self-teach (time feel, vibrato, phrasing, ear), shrink to bullet drills with aspirational pass lines ("sounds arranged rather than like a solo looking for somewhere to go").

7. **Pass criteria the learner can't check alone.** Many are subjective ("sounds like a sentence"), need another person ("Someone can hear which…"), or last months ("six monthly recordings", "three consecutive months"). They are written as outcome aspirations, not tests a learner can run alone in a session.

## 5. What is genuinely good (keep it)

- **Performance-based "Pass when" lines.** "You name any note on any string in about a second, and a tuner agrees with you twenty times in a row" is a real, teacher-free, falsifiable test. Lesson 1's "Go to the test at the bottom first" is a proper pretest that lets an expert skip.
- **Teaching by derivation, not lookup.** "Four then three, always" for triads; "A minor triad is a major triad with the third lowered one fret"; "Memorize one set plus 'add a fret past the G string'"; W-W-H-W-W-W-H applied on one string; the harmonized-scale "skip one, take the next" with the fret-count reason. This is the mechanism-first depth SKILL.md asks for.
- **Minimal-contrast pairs.** One lick in b3 and 3 ("Two frets moved. Everything else is identical."), Dorian vs Aeolian as one note, the b5 played passing and then held, Am→E vs Am→E7. These are strong perceptual-learning designs that just need audio.
- **Valid physical self-checks where they exist.** Unison bends ("the wobbling sound between them stops"), the tuner for note naming and bends, high-gain-plus-headphones for muting, slow-motion phone video for picking. These really work without a teacher.
- **Honest scoping inside lessons.** "Modes are oversold… Three of the seven are worth your time", "What to skip", "The honest caveat" in borrowed pentatonics, "This is a shortcut, not a substitute".
- **Signaling on the fretboard tool.** Roots filled and thirds ringed matches the book's main idea that "the third is the note that matters".
- **A daily planner with spacing intent,** a metronome with a real shuffle subdivision that matches the Subdivisions lesson, and personalization to the learner's stated models and rhythm background.

## 6. Root causes (as correctable behaviors)

1. **It took "teach me" to mean "write the complete syllabus."** The agent optimized for coverage ("Complete improvisation method", 96 lessons) instead of scoping one module to mastery. *Instruction:* "Scope the artifact to the first 1–3 objectives the learner can reach in the next few sessions. Offer the rest as a roadmap, not as built lessons."

2. **It wrote domain notation from memory and never checked it.** Tabs were produced as pattern-shaped text, and the agent's doubt shows up as hedges ("check each note against the scale before you trust this") instead of checks. *Instruction:* "Every worked example and answer key must be verified before shipping, by computing it (code, the artifact's own engine) or by an explicit item-by-item check. A hedge is not a substitute for a check. An answer key you haven't verified must not be presented as a self-check."

3. **It treated interactivity as tools to include, not activities tied to objectives.** It built a generic metronome, fretboard explorer and ear trainer, then wrote lessons as text beside them. *Instruction:* "For each objective, build the in-artifact activity where the learner produces the answer and gets feedback. A widget that only displays is decoration unless it asks the learner something."

4. **It let self-report stand in for assessment.** "Mark done" and "Reviewed" were the default completion model, and spaced review had no failure state. *Instruction:* "Wherever the artifact can check performance, it must. Where it can't, the self-check must use an external reference the learner can't fool, and every review must be able to record a miss that shortens the interval."

5. **It defaulted to text for a sensory or motor skill.** It described sounds in adjectives even though audio was available, and handed performance feedback to other apps. *Instruction:* "For any skill judged by ear or by the body, ask: what must the learner hear, and what will tell them whether *their* attempt matched? Play the targets in-page, and capture the learner's attempt (microphone pitch, timing) wherever the platform allows."

6. **It showed the answer, then asked for derivation.** The worked example and the exercise were the same figure. *Instruction:* "Never place the answer to an exercise before the learner's attempt. Use completion problems with hidden answers, and fade visibly across items."

7. **It revised by appending.** Each new idea became a new lesson, never merged, and counts and cross-references went stale. *Instruction:* "When revising, rewrite the affected structure. Then do one full read-through as the learner, checking counts, cross-references, duplicates, truncated sentences and any authoring notes."

8. **It wrote pass criteria as aspirations.** "Sounds arranged", "without thinking", "someone can hear". *Instruction:* "Each pass criterion must be a test the learner can run alone in one session, with an observable result. Long habits are not gates."
